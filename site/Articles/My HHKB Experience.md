### Overview
This article covers my seven days with my new HHKB Lite2 keyboard. I've
basically "journaled" any day I've wanted to update this blog so there may just
be two days, who knows!

Around a week ago, I ordered a HHKB Lite2 to be imported from Japan. For a long
time, I've wanted a HHKB, but when I checked, they were all ludicrously
expensive. Thats until my friend showed me how I could get one for just ~£15
(¥3,200). I bought one shortly after that.

Now the keyboard isn't a "proper" HHKB with the Topre switches and has membrane
instead, but I don't think that really matters as I prefer the sound of
membrane keyboards when programming anyways.

From the image, it looked very yellow (from sun damage) and I assumed that I
would probably want to [retrobright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrobright)
it with something like hydrogen-peroxide. It also was a Japanese layout but all
the US layouts were black and all of them were kind of gunky..

###  Day One: [_07/08/2026_]

#### Looks
Today I recieved the keyboard (surprisingly, it only took four days after
getting shipped from Japan)! My first impressions were that it's **very**
yellow; I initially thought that person who last owned it always had his
curtains open (how brave). It also sounded very pleasing. It was like the
average membrane sound but with a lot more _thock_--overall it was positive.

After a bit of time using it, I noticed that the yellowing was very evenly
coated and actually gave it a very pleasing creamy color. I personally think it
looks even better than the original, greyish colour; I don't think I'll
retrobright it.

I now have a new theory that the keyboard was just propped up on and outfacing
shelf of some store. There isn't a single hair underneath the keycaps and it's
in pristine condition!

#### Layout
I particularly enjoy the layout on this keyboard. It's basically QWERTY, but
with a few quirks.

The biggest ones are that the CapsLock is CTRL (Caps is now under Tab), there
are four new modifier keys and that it's in a Japanese layout.

While I used to host my ESC key on CapsLock, I think that moving control there
is fine but probably only on this keyboard (see the bit on the four modifiers).
Overall, CTRL with your pinky is very pleasing to use as it's a common key that
I don't have to reach for anymore.

The four extra modifiers for me are useless; I don't know Japanese so they
don't do much. That means I can remap them!

| Key       | Romanised       | Remap                                          |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------------------------------------|
| 半角/全角 | _Hankaku/Zenkaku_ | **Super** - It was still super but needed Fn to activate. Now it activates without the modifier |
| 無変換    | _Muhenkan_       | **ESC** - This is where I remapped it to; it's actually much easier to press than CapsLock but I still need to retrain myself |
| 変換      | _Henkan_         | **Space** - For these three keys, I have no idea what to map them to.. perhaps I'll do something later but I don't know for now. It also helps with me not missing space as it's a bit smaller now |
| 前候補    | _Mae Koho_       | **Space**                                     |
| カタカナ/ひらがな/ローマ字  | _Hiragana/Katakana/Romaji_ | **Space**          |

I think the Japanese input layout is actually very good for programming but I'm
still getting used to it. Some changes I like are:

<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
Colon and semi-colon are now separate--I don't have to press Shift when doing
something on Vim
</li>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
<strong>@</strong> also doesn't need a shift modifier--Emails, passwords and Vim macros are
even easier
</li>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
<strong>"</strong> is on 2! I don't normally use a British keyboard layout but when
I do, the one thing I appreciate is the placement of double-quotation marks
on 2.
</li>
</ul>

Some things I don't like:

<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
Underscore, one of the most important keys, is hidden far away next to shift!
I might just remap this to where <em>Hiragana/Katakana/Romaji</em> is..
</li>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
I think the SpaceBar is also too small (though I might just need some time to
		adjust my hand there)
</li>
<li style="list-style-type: hiragana;">
Single quotation mark on Shift-7. It seems kinda pointless and maybe even a
bit less natural when writing articles like this.
</li>
</ul>

These are all changes that I don't like being an English speaker, I presume
that Japanese speakers don't have some of these problems.
